# November 9 operational reports: Serbian cavalry and the retreat north

[ADFGVX Cipher](../../README.md) · [All cases](../README.md) · [Source certificate](../../experiments/p170_continuation/military_records.json) · [Reading conventions](../../docs/reading-conventions.md)

**A missing 20-coordinate prefix is recovered from another intercept.** The three-part report on p166 and a differently framed two-part report on p170 describe Serbian cavalry, the northward march of German formations, and transport preparations for the Alpenkorps. A p167 copy restores the beginning missing from p166 part 3. The clearer parallel also preserves the order footer.

Source recovery, transcription and editorial work: **icekylinx and GPT-6 Astra Pro**. The November 7–9 key is an already published key with its original provenance retained in the [key catalog](../../data/keys.json). These are additions to the source edition, not claims of a newly recovered historical key or a first historical decipherment. They do **not** solve the short **p170 A** challenge: this page's two parts are **p170 B**.

## Read the reports

The following German readings use the recorded edited witnesses and supply readable spacing and punctuation. Square brackets have the specific functions in the table below. The exact raw text and every ciphertext edit follow unchanged.

| Display form | Editorial category | What remains separate |
|---|---|---|
| `Serb[ische]` | Abbreviation expansion | The extra prose letters are not encoded positions. |
| `[in]` | Supplied grammar | The exact streams do not acquire a new word. |
| `Aben[d]meldung`, `Peterwa[r]dein`, `Fein[d]berührung`, `Lin[i]e` | Prose-only spelling normalization | The same-length edited witnesses still retain their missing letters. |
| `U[j]verbasz` | Priced source-erasure fill | J is a candidate coordinate fill, not an abbreviation expansion. |
| `[5. oder 6.]` | Linked key-cell alternatives | Every occurrence uses the same unresolved cell; the choices are not independent. |

### p166 A, with its p167 A continuation

**Readable German — the stated edited witness, with the linked 5/6 cell left unresolved:**

> O.H.L., O.P. Lage 11. Armee, 8.11., abends: Serb[ische] Kavallerie [in] Peterwardein und Fehertemplom eingerückt.
>
> Abmarsch 217. I.D. und **[5. oder 6.] R.D.** nach Norden ohne Feindberührung. Alpenkorps wird Umverbasz (Fragezeichen) zum Abtransport bereitgestellt.
>
> 219., 217. und **[5. oder 6.] R.D.** setzen 9.11. Marsch in Lin[i]e Nagybeskerek–Werschetz fort. O.K.M., 1a, No. 11319 op.

**Meaning.** An evening situation report describes Serbian cavalry entering Peterwardein and Fehertemplom. The 217th Infantry Division and a reserve division are marching north without enemy contact. The Alpenkorps is being assembled for onward transport at a place transmitted as `UMVERBASZ`, followed by the literal word `FRAGEZEICHEN` (“question mark”). The 219th and 217th divisions and the same reserve division continue their march on 9 November along the Nagybeskerek–Werschetz line. The footer is retained as `O.K.M., 1a, No.11319 op.`

The place spelling and the explicit question mark belong to the transmitted text. The p170 parallel offers the conditional reading **Ujverbasz**. The source spelling `NAGYBESKEREK` is retained. The raw edition leaves the reserve-division numeral as **5/6**. A contemporary French operational report supports **6th Reserve Division** in the historical-context layer below.

### p170 B, parts 1 and 2

**Readable German — the stated edited witness and prose-only additions identified below:**

> Aben[d]meldung: Serb[ische] Kavallerie [in] Peterwa[r]dein und Fehertemplom eingerückt. Abmarsch 217. I.D. und **[5. oder 6.] R.D.** nach Norden ohne Fein[d]berührung.
>
> Alpenkorps wird U[j]verbasz zum Abtransport bereitgestellt. 219., 217. und **[5. oder 6.] R.D.** setzen 9.11. Marsch in Linie Nagybeskerek–Werschetz fort.

**Meaning.** The two-part intercept repeats much of the operational information in the other format. It directly confirms the **217** and **219** numbers and the onward-march date **9.11**. The place-name letter `J` is a proposed fill of a received erasure. The headline, missing letters in the two words, and remaining transcription faults are explicitly editorial; the original two frames remain 118 and 119 plaintext positions.

These earlier German reports give additional source context for the units discussed in [p176b](../p176b.md). They do not identify that later message's unknown numeral cells by themselves, and they do not resolve the **5/6** reserve-division ambiguity in the present key.

## Historical identification: most likely the 6th Reserve Division

The French telegram and German calendar were already audited in the repository's [p176b unit-history review](../../data/reviews/p176b_unit_context.json). The new application here is to the newly source-edited p166/p167/p170 B reports and the **different November 7–9 square**. The earlier p176b plaintext and its 217/219/6 contextual reading retain their published attribution.

**A contemporary operational telegram supports the reading 6.** General Franchet d'Esperey's telegram **5.956/3**, sent from Salonique to Clemenceau and Foch on **19 November 1918 at 22:40**, describes the situation on **11 November**. Its paragraph on the reconstituted German 11th Army names the Alpenkorps, the 217th and 219th divisions around Nagy-Backskerek–Verschatz, and explicitly the **6th Reserve Division**. The relevant short passage reads: “217° — 219° D.I. Nagy-Backskerek—Verschatz) 6° D.I. réserve”. The matching formations and geography support the same identification in these German reports of 9 November; the French telegram is two days later in its reported situation and ten days later in transmission.

This is **document 39, printed p. 79** (document pp. 79–82; PDF p. 153) in *Documents diplomatiques français sur l'histoire du Bassin des Carpates, 1918–1932*, vol. I, *Octobre 1918 – Août 1919*, documents collected by Magda Ádám, György Litván and Mária Ormos, edited by Magda Ádám, Akadémiai Kiadó, Budapest, **1993**, ISBN 963 05 6659 1. The printed archival reference is **AG.4N58 c/63. d/10.** on p. 82. [Source PDF](https://real-eod.mtak.hu/19844/13/documents.pdf) · [Library catalog](https://real-eod.mtak.hu/19844/).

A retrospective battle calendar also agrees with this identification. *Ruhmeshalle unserer alten Armee*, vol. I, **fifth revised edition**, Verlag für Militärgeschichte und deutsches Schrifttum, Berlin/Fürstenwalde, printed p. **385**, places the 6th Reserve Division on a return march from the Balkans through Hungary from 6 November; p. **382** places the 5th Reserve Division at the Antwerp–Meuse position on 5–11 November. **The downloaded extract does not show a publication year**, so it is not attributed to the 1927 edition. This calendar is a later compilation from official archival material, distinct from the 1918 telegram. [Calendar extract, PDF pp. 43 and 46](https://denstorekrig1914-1918.dk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Ruhmeshalle_unsere_alten_Armee_5_Aufl_Bd-1_s_342-497s.pdf).

The [context certificate](../../experiments/p170_continuation/military_context.json) records the consequences separately:

| Layer | Reserve-division digit | Effect on the ciphertext |
|---|---|---|
| Published partial square and cipher arithmetic | `5` or `6`, at cell `VF` | Both alternatives retain exact global witnesses. |
| Historical identification supported by the French telegram | **`6`** at `VF`; consequently `5` at the other unused cell `XA` | **Zero additional coordinate edits** in each of the four readable frames. |

This selects one historical reading in four occurrences, not four independent key deductions. The original key in `data/keys.json` and all raw domains remain unchanged. The two complete squares still decrypt and re-encrypt the edited candidate frames exactly; the historical source, rather than cipher arithmetic, supplies the preference. Corrupted occurrences elsewhere still require their own source review.

The verifier checks **six records and 1,248 received source slots**, the source-derived prefix, every candidate's exact cipher arithmetic, and the fact that the reserve-division question marks in all four readable frames refer to the **same unassigned square cell**.
