# Mackensen's November 3 withdrawal report and its separately received ninth part

**Source edition and assembly audit: icekylinx and GPT-6 Astra Pro.**

This thirteen-part report assesses threats to the occupation army's rear
communications and proposes immediate withdrawal from Romania if a general
armistice is not expected soon. Its historical decipherment belongs to
**J. Rives Childs**; **George Lasry, Ingo Niebel, Nils Kopal and Arno Wacker**
published most of the reconstructed body in their 2016 paper, Table 3.
The present contribution is an exact source edition, repair accounting,
and the recovery of the report's assembly from separately received pieces.

There are **twelve surviving physical parts** here: eleven readable editions
and one heavily jammed ending retained as raw partial evidence. Part 2 is
absent and is not back-translated from the historical English translation.
The period square and column ranks remain the published **Nov1–3** key.

## Why part 9 belongs here

The consecutive copy on printed pp114–116 explicitly marks its ninth part
missing. Table 3 in the 2016 paper also leaves it missing. But the previous
source page contains **p113 B, explicitly labelled `9 TL`**. It shares the
`2000 (3)` message reference, the OHL destination, the Nov1–3 cipher key,
and the exact grammatical join from part 8 to part 10. Its receiving time is
9.38pm on November 5, after the November 4 main copy; the later receipt
explains why it is treated as a separate physical witness rather than
silently inserted into the page image.

Part 8 ends with the communications that had so far been disabled only
because of something; part 9 supplies the complete collapse of the Hungarian
army and disorganization of its railway system; part 10 continues with a
threat of enemy attack. The new ninth-part edition needs **zero changes
to its 216 observed coordinates** and yields 108 fixed plaintext labels.
This is a source-and-assembly result, not a claim of universal decipherment
priority over earlier historical work or the earlier unreviewed OCR candidate.

## The two short frames require different qualifications

Part 11 actually prints219 slots, although its header says220. An independent
manual retranscription confirms the four-character group `VGGV` at source
index 110. Testing **all 220 one-coordinate insertion boundaries** against
the readable template yields a unique zero-observed-substitution fit at
boundary 102. Masking the whole `WAFFENSTILLSTAND` word leaves the same minimum.
The five boundaries in or after the visibly short group,110–114, need 8,8,9,9
and 10 observed-coordinate changes under the complete template.

Thus the chosen model is arithmetically economical, but its missing-coordinate
location is **not visually witnessed by the short group**. A separate source
review found no transcription correction that removes this tension. The
edition does not infer how the historical printer or operator produced it.

Part 12 actually prints237 against 238. Its short group `DDGF` starts at 140.
All 238 single-loss boundaries were evaluated. Boundaries141,142,143 and 144
tie at three observed-coordinate changes; these positions fall within or
at the end of that short group. After masking `BESATZUNSHEER`, three of those
boundaries remain tied at two observed changes. None is promoted to a
uniquely witnessed missing-symbol location.

Both templates were formed after reading the source and the prior report.
These are exact conditional cost comparisons, not blind confirmation of
the missing-coordinate mechanism or historical wording. The certificate
retains all 458 gap comparisons, all tied raw readings, and the separate
short-group comparisons.

The source-derived part 12 wording is `ZEIT ZU ERWARTEN IST`, where Table 3
uses `ZEIT EINTRITT`. This edition follows the received coordinate evidence.
Similarly, source defects and the uncertain part 10 reference are retained
instead of importing every normalization from the later reconstruction.
