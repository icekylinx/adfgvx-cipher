# p111 B — Use radio for important messages

[Case guide](../README.md) · [ADFGVX Cipher](../../README.md) · [Source image](../../sources/excerpts/other_p111_B.png)

**Status:** A complete direct reading of a supplementary message. The unchanged
published `Nov1-3` key fixes **all 154 plaintext positions** and reproduces all
**308 source coordinates**. No source coordinate, key cell or message length
is changed.

## Most likely reading

> Fuer Chef Mittelmeerdivision, B. M. K., Nateko:
> Wegen Unsicherheit der Kabelverbindungen fuer wichtige Mel[d]ungen
> Funkspruch benutzen. Admiralstab 18266 D, roem. 2.

**Meaning:** Addressed to the chief of the Mediterranean Division, B.M.K. and
Nateko: because cable connections are unreliable, use radio for important
messages. The message closes with an Admiralty Staff reference, `18266 D`,
followed by the instruction to read `2` as a Roman numeral.

The body provides a direct reason for using radio: uncertainty about the
cable connections. It does not specify whether this was caused by physical
damage, interception, censorship or some other problem. `B. M. K.` and
`NATEKO` are retained as decoded abbreviations, without an unverified expansion.

The only supplied letter in the displayed reading is the bracketed `d` in
*Meldungen*. The raw text spells the word **`MELUNGEN`**. That spelling omission
is kept in the cryptographic transcript and is not counted as an extra
recovered position. Spacing, capitalization and punctuation are editorial.

## Source and dates

Printed p111 / PDF p114, section B. The page heading is **3 November 1918**,
and the internal stamp is `2315 (3.11)`. The reception header actually prints
`FUER NKJ, ASO, RAT v LP (2.54 a.m., November 5th); RICHI-308`.
The differing dates are visible in the source and are retained without
inferring a cause or silently changing either date.
