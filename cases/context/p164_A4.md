# p164 A4: a conditional reading of a contextual sibling

**Status: editorial reconstruction, not a source-preserving solution.** This unlisted fourth part supplies context for challenge messages p164a and p164b. It does not change their partial status. The source has 198 coordinate slots, including 13 erasures, although its header advertises 188. With the audited November 7–9 key and no repairs, 87 of 99 plaintext characters are fixed:

```text
VORG?OSSDX?A?GESESSEK?NX4X1XGXD?AGXGTIESSWES?LX?IRCOAUFGTAER0EREFGEGNERX?IY?OWURDE?OMGEGNER??SETSTX
```

IceKylin and GPT-6 Astra propose the following **conditional editorial reading**:

> Vorstoss der abgesessenen 4./1. G. Drag. stiess westl. MIRCO auf staerkeren Gegner. MIRCO wurde vom Gegner besetzt.

It describes an advance by dismounted cavalry, encountering stronger opposition west of an unidentified MIRCO, followed by a report that MIRCO was occupied by the enemy. Expanding `4./1. G. Drag.` as the 4th Squadron of the 1st Guard Dragoon Regiment is a contextual inference. The unit is attested in the US War Department's [1920 divisional history, p. 689](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/55620/55620-h/55620-h.htm#Page_689), under the 218th Division: its 1917 column names the Guard Dragoon regiment, while its 1918 column omits “Guard.” This association does not verify the message's wording or locate the squadron on the date of the intercept.

The full reading requires nine substitutions of known source coordinates and thirteen guessed erasure values. All nine printed glyphs were independently rechecked and support the literal transcription. These are substantive proposed repairs, not corrections to modern transcription.

## What this changes

The source fragment `A?GESESSEK?N` can be reconstructed as `ABGESESSENEN` with one known-coordinate substitution and three erasure fills. Consequently, its internal `ESSEK` substring is not sufficient evidence for identifying Osijek. MIRCO remains unidentified; no geographic substitution or expansion is adopted. The apparent height 828 in part 5 does not determine the interpretation.

## Geographic lead

[The geographic review](../../data/reviews/p164_geography.json) records Martin's 1920 regimental history: on October 24 it places twenty men of a closely matching 4th Squadron/1st Prussian Dragoon designation at Mosnac, near the later Miroc fighting. Its subsequent chronology and the different report date prevent a firm identification. Changing both `MIRCO` occurrences to `MIROC` would additionally alter three known source coordinates and one guessed fill. No such normalization is adopted. The relevant French daily journal is identified as an archive lead, with its entries still uninspected.
