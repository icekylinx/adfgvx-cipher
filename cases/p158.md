## Most likely component readings

p158 contains portions of two messages. Its most useful readable interpretation therefore has two components.

**Component B, from the p159/p168 witnesses:**

> Ueber 2. bayr. Gen.-[Kdo.], [Muenchen], an 7. [unit unclear] Div., [Landsberg an/am Lech]. Nicht durch Kabel. Armeeschluessel [doppelt]. Empfang [bestaetigen]. O.H.L. […] 11338 geh. […]

**Component C, from the p159/p168 witnesses:**

> Ueber 2. bair. Gen.-Kmd., Muenchen, an 4. bair. Div., [Innsbruck]. Nicht durch [Kabel]. [Armee-CHI] doppelt. Empfang [bestaetigen]. An O.H.L. […]

**Meaning:** Route messages through the Bavarian command in Munich, onward to the indicated formations or destinations. Do not use cable transmission; apply the specified army-key treatment and acknowledge receipt.

**Notes:** The place-name expansions and damaged unit/reference text remain editorial. `doppelt` describes the instruction found in the message; the contemporary report discusses a separate army double-transposition cipher and does not prove two successive ADFGVX encipherments. The composite-source identification is our contribution; order 11338 already has a wartime decipherment, credited below.

The prefix of p158 D corresponds to p159 B; the suffix corresponds to p159 C. Parallel copies on p168 C/D corroborate several source corrections. The mixing is already present in the historical page.

## Component readings

The two component messages concern routing through Munich to a destination plausibly read as Landsberg an/am Lech and to Innsbruck, avoiding cable transmission, double army-key treatment, and acknowledgment. Exact raw decipherments retain several garbles. The p159 B record has a duplicated five-symbol group, a missing group, two erased symbols, and a source disagreement; p159 C has a six-symbol group corrected to five using the parallel.

Three explicitly edited component versions pass exact re-encryption using only established square cells. The unresolved digits 5/6 are not assigned. Corrections and raw plaintext are in [the certificate](../data/certificates/p158.json).

## Earlier historical reading of a component

Childs's report dated **20 November 1918**, printed on p42 of *German Military Ciphers from February to November 1918* (1935), already gives a reading of order **11338**. Its SY→RVV via N4 header, 9 November date, and 21:03 time agree with the p168 retransmission. This prior decipherment is credited to the wartime record.

The report expands the garbled destination as `LANDWEHR REGIMENT; AN FECH`. The coordinate-derived `LA3DSBERGXAN0LECH` more closely suggests Landsberg an/am Lech; the exact expansion remains editorial. The surrounding report describes a separate **double-transposition army cipher**. It does not establish two complete ADFGVX encipherments.

Our contribution is the **p158 composite-source relationship and its explicit repair audit**, not the first recovery of order 11338. See [the historical comparison](../data/reviews/historical_component_prior.json) and [the primary report, printed p42](https://www.nsa.gov/Portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/publications/FOLDER_268/41784789082381.pdf).

The component bodies on **p168 C and D** are also discussed by **George Lasry,
Ingo Niebel, Nils Kopal and Arno Wacker** in §6.4 of
[*Deciphering ADFGVX messages from the Eastern Front of World War I*](https://doi.org/10.1080/01611194.2016.1169461),
printed p23 / author-PDF p24. That prior body credit also applies to the
parallel p159 B/C content. Their prose glosses the instruction as doubled
confirmation; this edition preserves the raw distinction between doubled
army-key treatment and receipt acknowledgment. Neither the earlier gloss nor
the current reading resolves every garbled unit and reference field. The
specific p158 composite identification remains the present contribution.
