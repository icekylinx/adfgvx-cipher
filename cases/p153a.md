## Most likely reading

The partial text uses linked unresolved fields. The displayed variables below are labels for unknown square cells, not recovered letters or digits. Word spacing does not choose a date or serial.

**Readable German — linked unresolved-cell variables; exact variable definitions remain below:**

```text
ANORDNUNGEN VOM <alpha>TEN <beta><beta>TEN NR <gamma><delta><gamma>
BEZIEHEN LASSEN SOWEIT NOCH NICHT GESCHEHEN
```

| Field in the displayed text | Exact unresolved structure | Display rule |
|---|---|---|
| Day-like field | `<alpha>` followed by the known `TEN` | No day value is supplied. |
| Month-like field | `<beta><beta>` followed by the known `TEN` | Both unknown positions are the same cell. |
| Serial-like field | `<gamma><delta><gamma>` | The outer positions are equal; the middle cell is distinct. |

The four labels identify four distinct square cells, not recovered digits.
Interpreting the repeated month as `11` is contextual; it would make
`<gamma>` different from `1`. The optimizer's arbitrary `NR121` rendering
must not be taken as a recovered number.

The preceding message context is missing. The closing phrase approximately
instructs that something be taken up or occupied insofar as this has not already
been done; the object cannot be supplied from this fragment alone.

### Date and serial: what can be narrowed

If these fields are an ordinary decimal day, month and three-character serial,
the repeated month must be **11**. If the order also predates reception on
8 November 1918, the day can be **2–8**. These are contextual assumptions, not
newly decrypted digits. They leave **392** possible date/serial assignments
when a leading zero in the serial is allowed, or **343** when it is forbidden.

The `<gamma>` character also occurs in the trailing text of [part 6](p153b.md).
That equality is fixed by the shared square even without a decimal
interpretation. The [linked-field analysis](../experiments/p153_extension/README.md)
records the exact domains and assumptions. A separate
[key-construction investigation](../experiments/p153_key_structure/README.md)
finds that the tested historical conventions and keyword searches cannot
justify filling the missing digits.

## Source

Printed page 153 / PDF page 156, section C5; November 8, 1918.
`FKR v CGH (11.55 p.m.); 2250; 5 TL; CHI-132`. The header means CGH to FKR.
This is part 5 of multipart traffic; [part 6](p153b.md) is also preserved.
No total part count is printed, and parts 1–4 were not located in the inspected
book. The [primary crop](../sources/excerpts/p153_CHI_parts5_6.png) and

An independent [full-page header review](../experiments/p153_headers/README.md)

finds no additional verified FKR/CGH part in that range. OCR misreads even the
known date and length, so this negative result is kept within those bounds.

## Key and verification

The [recovery report](../experiments/p153_recovery/README.md) gives the column
ranks, conservative nineteen-cell square, blind-search lineage and exact
source-coordinate maps. The [certificate](../data/certificates/p153.json)
retains both the raw fitted output and the conservative reading.

An [exhaustive conditional check](../experiments/p153_crib/README.md), implemented
independently in C and Python, finds only two equivalent partial-key
representations at width 18 given these sixty letters. The six masked positions
are not used as a digit crib. This establishes a restricted consistency result,
not unrestricted historical uniqueness. The [even-width](../experiments/p153_even_widths/README.md) and [odd-width](../experiments/p153_odd_widths/README.md) extensions cover every integer width from 14 through 26, totaling 3,492,604 distinct length-class layouts. Only width 18 fits that same crib.
