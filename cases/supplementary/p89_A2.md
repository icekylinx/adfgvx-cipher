# p89 A2 — ship-routing clause completed conditionally

**Original reading:** [Ayush Agarwal (@AyushAg1002), 2026-10-28](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver/issues/181), with review by **D. Bourdeau**. **Source, arithmetic and conditional completion:** icekylinx and GPT-6 Astra Pro. This source belongs to the wider Childs corpus, outside the original 22-entry challenge.

This continuation concerns port capacity and the routing of ships to
Nikolayev. The native source fixes **142 of 145 positions**. The proposed
words **`WELCHE SCHIFFE`** complete the three gaps with **three source-mask
fills and no observed-coordinate substitutions**. The historical meaning
and the phrase had already been proposed; the current contribution is their
explicit source-coordinate certificate and independent word support.

## Readable message

**Readable German — conditional fills in brackets; punctuation editorial:**

> … aufnahmefähig, Nikolajew. Auf Wunsch EZ Kiew bestimmt Hptm. Joel,
> V. O. FECH, Odessa, [w]elche S[c]hi[f]fe etwa wegen Eisenbahnlage nach
> Nikolajew zugehen haben.

The [preceding part](../military/black_sea_evacuation_routes.md#p88-c1)
orders traffic other than *Siegfried* to Odessa and ends with “and if this
is no longer …”. The opening here supplies “able to receive [it],
Nikolayev”. The following clause puts the selection of ships, taking the
railway situation into account, with Captain Joel in Odessa. This sentence
division reads `BESTIMMT HPTM JOEL` as verb and subject; it does not add a
missing verb or title. The abbreviation `EZ` and office string `V O FECH`
remain literal. The earlier readable interpretation used **Ex.** for `EZ`;
that was editorial display, not a priced `Z→X` coordinate repair.

## Exact completion and its limits

The original source separately labels **`2 TL RICHI-290`**. Its header is:

The source has **290 slots and 287 observed coordinates**, yielding 145
plaintext positions. It is not an arbitrary prefix of a combined
548-coordinate entry, and the body is not 130 characters long. The two
printed dashes in `V--GG` remain separate erased coordinate slots. The
first mark of group15 is damaged: D is visually preferred, but an E-like
appearance remains possible. The conservative transcription keeps it masked.

| 78 | `G?` → `58BDNW` | W | 176 | printed dash → F |
| 85 | `?D` → `248CIM` | C | 70 | disputed glyph → D |
| 88 | `?V` → `3BFKQS` | F | 177 | printed dash → A |

All indices are zero-based. **The native 142/145 count is unchanged.** The
proposed layer reaches **145/145**, keeping every observed coordinate and
the published Nov1–3 key. The D at source70 was already present in the
earlier attributed candidate, which fixed 143/145 positions; this edition
adds two dash fills and makes the entire three-fill cost explicit.

Two independent received words support the vocabulary: native **`WELCHES`**
in `WELCHES KALIBER` on [p86 A](../military/mg08_inventory.md) supplies
the same stem with a different grammatical ending; native **`SCHIFFE`**
appears on [p98 A2](../military/black_sea_disembarkation.md).
The latter uses the same key and concerns shipping from Kospoli to
Odessa/Nikolayev. Neither is an identical retransmission of this sentence.
There are **216 cryptographically compatible completions** of the three
missing halves. The certificate preserves all of them; the chosen phrase
is a conditional lexical reading, not a cipher-forced uniqueness result.
