## Coordinate-count constraint for a complete body

The source contains only **seven `D` coordinates**. If these 106 coordinates
are a complete 53-character body, the two adjacent dated squares impose the
following necessary bounds, where $n_c$ counts plaintext character $c$:

| Assumed square | Ordinary-letter count bound |
| --- | --- |
| Nov7–9 | $n_A+n_C+n_E+n_G+n_R+n_S+n_Z+2n_L\leq7$ |
| Nov10–12 | $n_A+n_D+n_F+n_G+n_K+n_N+n_O+n_S+n_W+2n_I\leq7$ |

Each character contributes one `D` for row D, one for column D, or two at
DD. Transposition preserves that total, so these bounds allow every column
width, rank and readout direction. The remaining relevant cells are digits:
for Nov7–9, adding $n_0+n_2+n_3$ gives equality; for Nov10–12, the
unassigned digit at FD consumes any remainder. No unresolved digit assignment
can weaken either bound.

The [source-bound evidence record](../experiments/open_targets/p170_coordinate_budget.json)
also compares every 53-character window from the two pinned German books used
in the [p73 count study](../experiments/open_targets/P73_HISTOGRAM.md).
None meets either bound under joined or sentence-X normalization. For actual
operational references, the known ordinary letters in unrestored p155 A1 cost
17–23 Ds per such window under Nov7–9; p179 B costs 21–29 under Nov10–12.
Unknown characters are charged zero. These comparisons are descriptive, not
p-values.

Actual telegrams also show why this is only a necessary condition. The first
53 raw characters of [p121 A1](radio/nov4_repetition_and_connections.md),
which include eleven date and reference digits, cost exactly seven ordinary-letter
Ds under the Nov10–12 square. Seventy-two of that square's 120 digit
completions also give exactly seven Ds in total. This example was actually
received under Nov4–6; it is not a p170 candidate. Its full six-coordinate
histogram would still require at least eighteen substitutions to match p170,
under every completed square and coordinate permutation. Passing one count
bound does not establish ciphertext compatibility.

The book comparison supports lowering the research priority of a complete
body of ordinary German prose under those squares. It **does not exclude either key, prove truncation, or recover
plaintext**. Other squares, unusual vocabulary, numerals, code traffic or
another language remain possible.

## Attack status

The [known-key truncation experiment](../experiments/known_key/P170_TRUNCATION_NOTE.md) checked **1,291,584 contiguous placements** across 21 keys and fixed total-length bounds. No plaintext was accepted. Its long-suffix control ranks the planted geometry highly while determining zero plaintext letters, so the search cannot exclude truncation. Upstream training overlap with a control body is disclosed.

Other bounded component searches are described in [the experiment index](../experiments/README.md). A high-scoring German-looking fragment is not treated as a recovered message.

The [completed open-target campaign](../experiments/open_targets/README.md)
adds 35 arbitrary-column-order cases under five anchored squares at widths
16–22, plus independent row/column relabeling under 24 fixed key hypotheses.
No accepted reading emerged. Its fresh relabeling control recovers all 53
plaintext positions, while separate unknown-square component controls fail;
both outcomes remain available. These experiments do not resolve the missing
length and provenance information in the header.

A [direct source-similarity search](../experiments/source_similarity/README.md)
finds only a 20-coordinate local resemblance to p110 C. Image review does not
extend it: fitting the entire 106-coordinate target still costs 58 unit edits.
The search retrieves a deliberately shortened and damaged source control, but
establishes no new p170 parallel in the 788-block OCR corpus.
