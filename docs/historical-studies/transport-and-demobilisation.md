# Vehicles, equipment and the last men at the station

[Historical studies](README.md) · [Printed-page editions](../../cases/pages/README.md)

The transport messages of 13–15 November 1918 describe withdrawal as a succession of practical decisions: obtain vehicles, prepare a departure, postpone it, select what to load, and keep a station working while equipment leaves. A report that a truck has departed ends with the sender's intention to remain with several men until Sunday. Departure of the vehicle and departure of the personnel were separate events.

Several texts carry the name Seifert and recur on the CM/CZ radio link. Their relationship is supported most firmly where the ciphertexts preserve parallel versions of the same message. Elsewhere, a shared name, vocabulary or station pair connects the correspondence without establishing one convoy or a complete personal itinerary. In the headers, `CM v CZ` means CZ transmitting to CM; a radio sender can relay another person's message. The editions preserve both header dates and the times written separately beneath them.

## Preparing to leave, then waiting

The damaged p185 D instruction formerly left its vehicle wording unread. Our reconstruction supplies **LASTAUTOS** and **ABMARSCHBEREITSEIN** within the retained five-coordinate-loss model:

> […] marschiert Lastautos morgen 1500 ueber DHFN. Abmarschbereit sein. […] Seifert.

The vehicle noun and readiness instruction require three changes to observed coordinates, one fill of a printed invalid glyph and two fills within the missing group. The resulting edition fixes 95 of 99 positions. Four gap locations remain equally economical under the lexical comparison, so the passage is a conditional reconstruction. Its opening `STATMET`, unusual syntax and damaged closing command remain in the [exact edition](../../cases/pages/185.md#source-p185-d); neither the station designation nor `DHFN` supplies an identified geographical origin.

On 14 November, p193 C reports a postponement: **“Abmarsch verschoben. Kein Grund zur Beunruhigung.”** Departure will take place later in the agreed manner, while `DHFN` remains `BETR bereit`, interpreted as operational readiness. The message's purpose includes reassurance as well as rescheduling. Its heading identifies CM as the transmitting station but leaves the recipient blank. It therefore cannot by itself establish that this postponement answers p185 D, although both messages discuss departure, retain `DHFN` and close with Seifert in the edited reading.

The p194 A/p196 B parallel adds a destination: **“Marschziel Hanau.”** It directs departure on Friday in the agreed manner and tells the recipient to keep vehicles ready. The two receptions supply complementary letters in *Freitag*, *bereithalten* and the signature; their 174-coordinate bodies differ at only three positions. They preserve one closely repeated instruction, rather than evidence for two separate departures on their different printed dates.

This sequence supports a history of preparation and revised expectations. It does not yield an exact delay. The planned `1?00` and the truck report's `1?00` use different unresolved key cells, while p198 C records receipt at `13.03 p.m.` beside an originating `13.45`. Those inconsistencies prevent turning the surviving messages into a continuous timetable. The separate [linked-field analysis](../../experiments/transport_followup/linked_fields.json) records the numerical relationships; the distinction between an order, a postponement and a departure report is already clear from the verbs.

## Selecting vehicles and retaining equipment

The short incoming reports give substance to the preparations. On 14 November, p193 E announces a small sprung cart available for attachment and mentions a car. On 15 November, the reverse-direction p189 E message states that vehicles are coming and trailers are present. Its reading of **two vehicles and two trailers** depends on the separately established conditional digit assignment; the lifting pump is described as made of sheet metal. These reports document local availability and expectation. They do not identify a single vehicle shared by every message.

The p197 D/p198 E loading instruction is more selective: **“Nur besten Anhaenger beladen”**—load only the best trailer—and keep the petrol pump ready. The two damaged receptions require an edited composite, with the trailer and pump repairs accounted for in the [full edition](../../cases/pages/197.md#source-p197-d). The wording establishes a choice among matériel. It does not explain whether the choice arose from carrying capacity, mechanical condition or another restriction. The pump named here is a *Benzinpumpe*; equating it with p189 E's *Hebepumpe* would add an equipment identification absent from the messages.

A second parallel orders two `US` apparatuses loaded for Darmstadt and requires the station to remain operationally ready. Darmstadt and Hanau thus appear as distinct destinations in the correspondence. The apparatus order goes to CM in one reception and SY in the other, whereas the Hanau instruction is on the CZ-to-CM link. The texts establish separate transport requirements; they do not establish that apparatus and personnel travelled together. Nor does either message name a workshop to which all the traffic can be assigned.

## Service continuity and the men remaining

The truck-departure report makes the division between moving matériel and remaining personnel explicit:

> Lastauto 1?00 abgefahren. Ich bleibe mit ? Mann bis Sonntag. Alles Gute, auf Wiedersehen.

The completed action is the truck's departure. The stay until Sunday is the sender's stated intention. The number of men remains unread, and the signature requires the separately recorded Seifert repair. The report contains no arrival at Hanau, and no direct statement that these particular men were retained to operate the station. Read beside the apparatus order and p193 C's readiness instruction, however, it shows how withdrawal could proceed in stages while a reduced presence remained. Continued communications and removal of equipment had to be arranged within the same period.

The earlier Seifert service traffic supplies a related administrative setting. [p168 A](../../cases/pages/168.md#source-p168-a) reports soldiers' council supervision while insisting that station personnel remain under the sender's control and operation is unchanged. [p172 A](../../cases/pages/172.md#source-p172-a) reports the destruction of records but continued personal custody of the cipher keys. These statements distinguish political supervision, possession of documents and the ability to keep transmitting. The station-control reading was already published by Lasry, Niebel, Kopal and Wacker; our edition supplies the source comparisons and the connection with the later transport material.

The short enquiries of 12 November show how closely such arrangements depended on news of individual movements. [p183 B](../../cases/pages/183.md#source-p183-b) asks to be told when Seifert has arrived; [p183 C](../../cases/pages/183.md#source-p183-c) reports his departure; [p183 E](../../cases/pages/183.md#source-p183-e) asks where the car is. Their repetition across the manuscript measures the circulation of information, not a corresponding number of journeys. The later departure and loading reports extend this picture from personal whereabouts to the allocation of vehicles, apparatus and men. They document the practical work of withdrawal, while discharge from military service itself lies outside these particular texts.
