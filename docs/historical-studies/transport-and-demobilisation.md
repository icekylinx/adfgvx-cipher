# Vehicles, equipment and the last men at the station

[Historical studies](README.md) · [Printed-page editions](../../cases/pages/README.md)

The transport messages of 13–15 November 1918 describe withdrawal as a succession of practical decisions: obtain vehicles, prepare a departure, postpone it, select what to load, and keep a station working while equipment leaves. A report that a truck has departed ends with the sender's intention to remain with several men until Sunday. The truck's reported departure and the sender's intended stay are distinct stages of withdrawal.

Several texts carry the name Seifert and recur on the CM/CZ radio link. Parallel ciphertexts preserve repeated instructions; shared names, vocabulary and station pairs connect the wider correspondence without defining a single convoy or personal itinerary. In the headers, `CM v CZ` means CZ transmitting to CM, although a radio sender can relay another person's message.

## Departure orders and postponement

The p185 D reconstruction supplies **LASTAUTOS** and **ABMARSCHBEREITSEIN** within the retained five-coordinate-loss model:

> […] marschiert Lastautos morgen 1500 ueber DHFN. Abmarschbereit sein. […] Seifert.

The vehicle noun and readiness instruction require three changes to observed coordinates, one fill of a printed invalid glyph and two fills within the missing group. The reading fixes 95 of 99 positions but retains four equally economical gap locations. Its opening `STATMET`, unusual syntax and damaged closing command remain in the [conditional edition](../../cases/pages/185.md#source-p185-d); neither the station designation nor `DHFN` identifies a geographical origin.

On 14 November, p193 C reports a postponement: **“Abmarsch verschoben. Kein Grund zur Beunruhigung.”** Departure will take place later in the agreed manner, while `DHFN` remains `BETR bereit`, interpreted as operational readiness. CM transmitted the message, but its recipient field is blank. Despite the shared departure subject, `DHFN` and Seifert signature, its relationship to p185 D as a reply remains uncertain.

The p194 A/p196 B parallel adds a destination: **“Marschziel Hanau.”** It directs departure on Friday in the agreed manner and tells the recipient to keep vehicles ready. The two receptions supply complementary letters in *Freitag*, *bereithalten* and the signature; their 174-coordinate bodies differ at only three positions. They preserve one closely repeated instruction, rather than evidence for two separate departures on their different printed dates.

The length of the delay remains unresolved. The planned `1?00` and the truck report's `1?00` use different unresolved key cells, while p198 C records receipt at `13.03 p.m.` beside an originating `13.45`. These unresolved and conflicting fields, documented in the [linked-field analysis](../../experiments/transport_followup/linked_fields.json), prevent a continuous timetable.

## Vehicle selection and equipment destinations

On 14 November, p193 E announces a small sprung cart available for attachment and mentions a car. On 15 November, the reverse-direction p189 E message reports vehicles coming and trailers present. Its reading of **two vehicles and two trailers** depends on a conditional digit assignment; the lifting pump is described as made of sheet metal.

The p197 D/p198 E loading instruction is selective: **“Nur besten Anhaenger beladen”**—load only the best trailer—and keep the petrol pump ready. The two damaged receptions require an edited composite, with the trailer and pump repairs accounted for in the [full edition](../../cases/pages/197.md#source-p197-d). The reason for selecting the trailer is unstated. The pump is a *Benzinpumpe*; its identity with p189 E's *Hebepumpe* remains unestablished.

A second parallel orders two `US` apparatuses loaded for Darmstadt and requires the station to remain operationally ready. The apparatus order goes to CM in one reception and SY in the other; the Hanau instruction travels on the CZ-to-CM link. The destinations and station pairs remain distinct. The messages identify neither a common transport for apparatus and personnel nor a shared workshop.

## Station readiness and remaining personnel

The truck-departure report makes the division between moving matériel and remaining personnel explicit:

> Lastauto 1?00 abgefahren. Ich bleibe mit ? Mann bis Sonntag. Alles Gute, auf Wiedersehen.

The truck's departure is reported as complete; the stay until Sunday is intended. The number of men remains unread, and the signature requires the recorded Seifert repair. The report gives neither an arrival at Hanau nor a duty for the remaining men. Alongside the apparatus order and p193 C's readiness instruction, it distinguishes vehicle movement, intended personnel departure and the requirement for continued station service.

The earlier Seifert service traffic supplies a related administrative setting. [p168 A](../../cases/pages/168.md#source-p168-a) reports soldiers' council supervision while insisting that station personnel remain under the sender's control and operation is unchanged. [p172 A](../../cases/pages/172.md#source-p172-a) reports the destruction of records but continued personal custody of the cipher keys. These statements distinguish political supervision, possession of documents and the ability to keep transmitting. The station-control reading was already published by Lasry, Niebel, Kopal and Wacker; our edition supplies the source comparisons and the connection with the later transport material.

The short enquiries of 12 November concern individual movements. [p183 B](../../cases/pages/183.md#source-p183-b) asks to be told when Seifert has arrived; [p183 C](../../cases/pages/183.md#source-p183-c) reports his departure; [p183 E](../../cases/pages/183.md#source-p183-e) asks where the car is. Repeated receptions trace the circulation of these notices. The later departure and loading reports turn from personal whereabouts to the allocation of vehicles, apparatus and men. They concern the practical work of withdrawal; discharge from military service lies outside these texts.
