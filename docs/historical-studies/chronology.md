# A chronology of withdrawal, control and return

[Historical studies](README.md) · [Complete editions by printed page](../../cases/pages/README.md)

The traffic follows withdrawal as a series of practical and political decisions: where ships should unload, how troops would obtain food and cash, who controlled a radio station, and whether men could continue home. The entries below connect those decisions across the collection. Each source link opens the complete edition for its printed page at the relevant ciphertext record.

All dates are in **1918**. **Message** identifies a dated originating field or an internal report date; **interception** identifies the receiving record; **reported event** identifies a date stated in the body. A **proposed effective date** records an intended arrangement. These dates need not coincide. The action verbs distinguish reports, instructions, requests and promises; material uncertainties are identified beside the affected entry.

The strongest documentary links are the explicit amendment of **FR 52751**, the successive *Corcovado* instructions, and the distinction between the 25 November appeal and the later Berlin council's assurances. The crown requests and bank reply form a closely related financial sequence, but their surviving references do not prove that they are a single complete exchange. The Arad provision request, coal report and equipment dispatch likewise expose different constraints on movement without establishing that they concern the same train, consignment or formation.
