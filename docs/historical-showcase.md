## The p153 fragments: a newly recovered shared key

On 2 November 2026 we recovered the **shared eighteen-column key and a nineteen-letter partial square** for the two p153 CHI fragments. Part 5 now yields **60 of 66 positions** without any source-coordinate change. The same key supports the reconstruction in part 6:

> Munitionsbestand der Nachhutartillerie melden.

“Report the ammunition inventory of the rearguard artillery.” This second reading requires twelve erased-coordinate fills and two source corrections; its closing fields remain incomplete. The messages' location is still unknown. Our result is the first documented key and reconstruction identified in the [reviewed public literature](historical-studies/README.md#what-this-edition-contributes), which credits Norbert's earlier proposal to analyse the fragments together.

## Arad: provisions for 80,000 men and 30,000 animals

A report received on 9 November requests provision trains to Arad and action through the Hungarian government. Its strength statement reads **“STAEKE 80000 Mann, 30000 Tiere.”** The report makes the scale of the withdrawal's supply requirements explicit: food and fodder had to be arranged while transit itself was being negotiated.

Our identification of a second reception restores all eleven coordinates missing from the earlier p159 reconstruction and fixes **80000** from observed ciphertext pairs. The two copies also repair complementary gaps. The separate fourteen-day and ten-day fields retain their uncertain relationship.

## Thirty million crowns by aircraft—and notes small enough to spend

The 9 November request asks for **“dreissig Millionen Kronen mittels Riesenflugzeug nach Brasso”**: thirty million crowns by giant aircraft to Brasso. It specifies a mixture of large and small notes for pay and purchases in Hungary. The next day's request proposes a daily million by ordinary aircraft; a later reply describes an initial five-million transfer through banks in Vienna and Kronstadt amid a shortage of banknotes.

Our source editions reconnect the multipart correspondence. A complete later reception also supplies the nineteen-coordinate tail missing from the instruction to send the money in the smallest possible notes. It resolves the location of the interruption and corrects one earlier conjectural value. The records document the requests and payment arrangements; they contain no confirmation of a completed airlift.

## From supplying the Caucasus to collecting its troops

The Sevastopol supply office reports dispatching **16,400 kg of sugar, 16,300 kg of hard feed, 24,200 kg of potatoes, 4,900 kg of millet and 8,700 kg of fresh vegetables** to the Caucasus delegation on 27 October. All 148 positions of the surviving first part decipher without repair, including every quantity.

The following fortnight's traffic changes disembarkation ports and expects *Asgard* at Poti to collect troops. Hopman's diary independently records the ship arriving at Sevastopol on 7 November and departing for Poti on 9 November. Comparison of the telegram and diary identifies successive stages of the movement and shows the same maritime routes being used for withdrawal.

## Corcovado: the terms of a proposed sale

The proposed sale of *Corcovado* to Turkey values the ship at **10 million**, with the currency unstated. Inventory is assessed separately; personal possessions and coal are excluded. The terms address enemy seizure, interest and recovery of the vessel for nonpayment. A subsequent message reports a Turkish crew and captain, while a later instruction makes transfer dependent on payment.

Our six-record edition reconstructs the proposal and its sequel. A contemporary Lloyd's Register entry then traces the ship under the name *Sueh* and a Turkish flag to a cross-referenced supplement recording *Corcovado* in French Government ownership. This establishes a later registered status; completion of the intercepted ten-million transaction remains unconfirmed.

## Arrival enquiries, supper and a farewell

A 14 November service message asks **“Wollen warmes Abendbr. bereiten. Wiev. Koepfe.”** The operators propose a warm evening meal and need to know how many people to expect. Nearby traffic postpones a departure; other messages ask where the car is or whether a colleague has arrived. The latter enquiry now has complete pair support from two complementary damaged receptions.

On 15 November, a short intact telegram reads **“ABFAHR. Wiedersehen. Gruesse, Kramm. Opitz.”** All thirty-seven positions decipher without repair. These records preserve the everyday communications of personnel using the network, alongside the command and political traffic for which the collection is better known.
