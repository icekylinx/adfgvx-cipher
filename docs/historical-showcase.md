# What the recovered messages reveal

[ADFGVX Cipher](../README.md) · [Four historical studies](historical-studies/README.md) · [Complete catalogue](../cases/README.md)

**An army needs food. A treasury needs banknotes. A ship needs a destination. Someone wants to know how many people are coming for supper.** The intercepted ADFGVX traffic preserves the ending of the war through decisions large and small. These selections bring its strongest historical material to the foreground, with the new source recoveries that make it readable and trustworthy.

The research and edition are by **icekylinx and GPT-6 Astra Pro**. Our contributions include a new shared key for the p153 fragments, independent-copy restorations, reconstructed message sequences and a connected, verifiable source edition. Earlier decipherers retain their specific credit. [Contribution and literature review](historical-studies/README.md#what-this-edition-contributes).

## 80,000 men, 30,000 animals, and trains to Arad

> STAEKE 80000 Mann, 30000 Tiere.

The report received on 9 November asks for provisions and supply trains to Arad, with action through the Hungarian government. The figures give the retreat its physical scale: tens of thousands of people and animals had to eat while their route home was being negotiated.

**Our second-copy recovery settles the formerly uncertain 80000 directly from observed ciphertext pairs** and supplies all eleven coordinates missing from the earlier p159 reconstruction. The message's fourteen-day and ten-day fields remain separate; their exact relationship is not supplied. This is a particularly valuable discovery because the restored evidence changes a substantive historical quantity.

## Thirty million crowns by giant aircraft

> … dreissig Millionen Kronen mittels Riesenflugzeug nach Brasso …

The 9 November request combines a dramatic means of transport with a very ordinary requirement: send a useful mixture of large and small notes. Pay and purchases in Hungary required cash at the destination. A 10 November message asks for a daily million by ordinary aircraft, and a later reply describes an initial five-million transfer through banks in Vienna and Kronstadt amid a shortage of banknotes.

Our editions restore the connected source parts. A separately found complete witness also restores the interrupted instruction to send the money in the smallest possible notes, replacing an absent nineteen-coordinate tail with surviving evidence. The airlifts remain requests, not documented completed flights.

## Potatoes outward, troops home

The Sevastopol supply office reports dispatching **16,400 kg of sugar, 16,300 kg of hard feed, 24,200 kg of potatoes, 4,900 kg of millet and 8,700 kg of fresh vegetables** to the Caucasus delegation on 27 October. Every quantity and every letter of the surviving first part deciphers without a change.

Other traffic soon redirects disembarkation from Constanza to Odessa and expects *Asgard* at Poti to collect troops. Hopman's independently preserved diary places *Asgard*'s arrival at Sevastopol on 7 November and its departure for Poti on 9 November. The juxtaposition turns a collection of isolated shipping records into a history of an overseas supply system being redirected toward evacuation.

## A steamer for sale, with the risk of seizure

The proposed *Corcovado* sale fixes the ship's price at **10 million**, without naming the currency. Inventory is valued separately; personal possessions and coal are excluded. The terms address seizure by Germany's enemies, interest and recovery of the vessel for nonpayment. The next day's message reports a Turkish crew and captain.

This is the commercial anatomy of withdrawal: an effort to preserve an asset's value and decide who would bear the risk. Our six-record source edition recovers the sale proposal and its immediate sequel; a later instruction makes transfer dependent on the payment condition. The texts do not establish a completed sale.

## What “these formations” meant

An order to dissolve military formations and send the remaining personnel home gains a much sharper meaning when its first part is restored. The preceding message names field hospitals and medical companies in Palestine and reports them lost **except for parts of their personnel**.

Our source comparison connects the intact repeated second part at p83 with the complete two-part sequence at p79. It recovers the missing medical antecedent of the administrative order. The source does not give a casualty total, but it does tell us what the command intended to do with the personnel who remained.

## “How many people?” and a farewell

> Wollen warmes Abendbr. bereiten. Wiev. Koepfe.

The proposed warm-supper query of 14 November sits beside reassurance about a postponed departure. Other brief records ask where the car is or whether anything is happening at the other end. On 15 November, an intact message reads:

> ABFAHR. Wiedersehen. Gruesse, Kramm. Opitz.

The farewell's thirty-seven characters need no cipher repair. The supper query has explicit local corrections, and the short forms stay short. Our edition gives these ordinary exchanges the same source attention as command orders. They preserve the people using the network without inventing biographies for the names.

## Women and children: an enquiry, not an arrival list

A late-November message asks whether reports of German civilians leaving Constantinople are correct, where they went and what is known of their whereabouts. It specifies principally women and children and seeks verification through the Swedish legation. The headcount remains unresolved.

The wider diplomatic record independently documents a German appeal to let civilians remain in Turkey after the armistice. Our reconstructed four-part source makes the enquiry and the report of that appeal readable together. Its historical force lies in the questions themselves: authorities were trying to find people and determine whether they could stay or travel.

## From negotiating withdrawal to asking for bearable internment

Mackensen's 25 November appeal warns that disarmament and internment could provoke mass flight, misery and resistance. A letter originating on 29 November tells his soldiers' council that efforts to avoid internment have failed and promises to seek tolerable conditions and an eventual safe return.

These major texts were already known to Childs and published in Lasry and collaborators' historical study. Our contribution is their checked German source edition and the recovery of damaged or omitted parts. In the appeal, the restored wording distinguishes merely acknowledging a note while awaiting a decision from accepting its demands. Beside the newly strengthened Arad report, the letters complete the arc from provisioning a march to negotiating the terms of enforced delay.

## How another voice returned: the new p153 key

The two p153 CHI fragments had remained open in the reviewed public record. On 2 November 2026 we recovered their **shared eighteen-column key and a nineteen-letter partial square**. The first fragment now yields sixty of sixty-six positions without changing its source. The second supports the reconstructed instruction:

> Munitionsbestand der Nachhutartillerie melden.

“Report the ammunition inventory of the rearguard artillery.” Its damaged body requires twelve erased-coordinate fills and two explicit source corrections; the date, reference and closing fields remain incomplete. The fragment's geographical setting has not been established. This is the first documented reconstruction identified by our review of the [literature and public discussion](historical-studies/README.md#what-this-edition-contributes), with Norbert's earlier joint-analysis suggestion credited separately.
