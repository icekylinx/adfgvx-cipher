## Scope cautions

The paper's p140 A, p146 A, p151 C, p172 C, p180 E, p227 D and p230 E
references must not be transferred to unrelated sections on the same
page. In particular, they do not identify p140 B (Glatz), the p146 Kerch
challenge, p151 D2 (courier), p172 A/B (Seifert), p180 A3 or p230 A
(operational reports), or p227 B (routing/key procedure).

Table 3 uses **Childs 1978, p212** for a missing retreat-plan part. This
is a memoir citation, not an Appendix-B p212 intercept. The same table
cites **Chi00 p213** for a translated gap in part 13, while identifying
the cryptogram as p116. That reference is internally problematic and
must not automatically confer body identification on a separate p213
intercept.

Earlier interpretation does not require adopting every earlier gloss.
The paper paraphrases p168 C/D in terms of doubled confirmation; the
current source edition separately preserves doubled army-key treatment
and receipt acknowledgment. Likewise, the current p199 cargo wording
should be established from its source, while retaining the paper's body
priority.

No explicit body reference was located for the current p163/p164 HQ
withdrawal family, p180 A3/p217 A/p230 A operational family, p187 D1
aviation message, p191 A boots request, or p200 A Siedlce report.
This absence is **not a first-decipherment claim**. The paper reports
broad earlier decryption work, and all editions must retain the key and
source provenance even when no individual body is printed there.
